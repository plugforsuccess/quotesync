# Insurance Newsroom Editorial Standards

**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** January 9, 2026
**Review Frequency:** Annually
**Compliance Owner:** [To Be Assigned]

---

## Mission Statement

Provide accurate, timely, and helpful insurance information to consumers without offering legal advice, claims recommendations, or exhibiting carrier bias. Our newsroom builds trust through factual reporting and education.

---

## Core Editorial Principles

1. **Accuracy First** - Every fact must be verifiable and sourced
2. **Consumer Focus** - Content serves readers, not sales agenda
3. **Regulatory Compliance** - Never cross into legal advice or inducements
4. **Transparency** - Clearly disclose sources, affiliations, and limitations
5. **Accessibility** - Write for 8th-grade reading level

---

## Content Categories & Rules

### 1. LITIGATION (Red Flag Category - High Risk)

**Allowed:**
- ✅ Factual reporting of court rulings with source citation
- ✅ Summary of publicly filed lawsuits
- ✅ Explanation of legal precedents affecting insurance

**PROHIBITED:**
- ❌ Speculation on fault or liability
- ❌ Advice on whether to sue or settle
- ❌ Prediction of case outcomes
- ❌ Endorsement of attorneys or law firms
- ❌ Encouragement to pursue litigation

**Example - GOOD:**
> "The Georgia Supreme Court ruled in *Smith v. ABC Insurance* (2024) that diminished value claims must be honored within 30 days of appraisal. [Link to court document]. This ruling clarifies rights for Georgia policyholders whose vehicles depreciate after repairs."

**Example - BAD:**
> "If your car is worth less after an accident, you should sue your insurer. Based on the Smith case, you'll probably win and get thousands of dollars."

**Why This Matters:**
Legal advice without a license violates state bar regulations. Agents can explain rulings but cannot advise specific legal action.

---

### 2. LAW & POLICY CHANGES

**Allowed:**
- ✅ Summary of new insurance laws (cite statute number)
- ✅ Explanation of policy changes
- ✅ Consumer rights education
- ✅ Timeline for when changes take effect

**PROHIBITED:**
- ❌ Legal advice ("you should file a complaint with the DOI")
- ❌ Interpretation of ambiguous laws (defer to attorney)
- ❌ Encouragement to violate policy terms

**Required Elements:**
- Statute or regulation citation
- Effective date
- Link to official government source
- Disclaimer if law varies by county/municipality

**Example - GOOD:**
> "Georgia HB 123 (effective Jan 1, 2026) requires insurers to offer diminished value coverage as an optional endorsement. Policyholders can add this coverage at renewal. [Link to Georgia General Assembly]. Pricing varies by carrier."

**Example - BAD:**
> "New Georgia law lets you force your insurer to pay for diminished value. Call the DOI and demand this coverage immediately."

---

### 3. ACCIDENT & CLAIMS (Highest Volume Category)

**Allowed:**
- ✅ General claims process education
- ✅ Timeline explanations
- ✅ Document checklists
- ✅ "What to expect" guides

**PROHIBITED:**
- ❌ Specific claims advice ("your case is worth $X")
- ❌ Fault attribution ("the other driver was clearly at fault")
- ❌ Claim valuation or settlement recommendations
- ❌ Encouragement to misrepresent facts
- ❌ Disparagement of adjusters or carriers

**Red Flag Keywords (Auto-Block in System):**
The following phrases trigger automatic compliance review:
- "You should sue"
- "Deny the offer"
- "They are low-balling you"
- "This is worth $X" (specific dollar amounts for claims)
- "Hire an attorney"
- "Commit insurance fraud" (obviously)
- "Inflate your claim"
- "Don't tell the adjuster"

**Example - GOOD:**
> "After an accident, Georgia law requires you to notify your insurer within a reasonable time (typically 24-72 hours). Here's a checklist:
> - Exchange information with other driver
> - Document the scene (photos, witness info)
> - File police report if required
> - Contact your insurer
> - Follow their claims process
>
> Your insurer will assign an adjuster to assess damages."

**Example - BAD:**
> "Your car damage looks like $5,000 easy. The adjuster will low-ball you at $2,500. Deny their first offer and threaten to sue. That usually gets you the full amount."

---

### 4. DATA & STATISTICS

**Allowed:**
- ✅ Industry statistics with proper attribution
- ✅ Trend analysis
- ✅ Cost comparisons
- ✅ Safety data from government sources

**PROHIBITED:**
- ❌ Unverified statistics
- ❌ Cherry-picked data without context
- ❌ Misleading charts or graphs
- ❌ Outdated data (>2 years old without disclosure)

**Source Quality Tier System:**

**Tier 1 (Always Acceptable):**
- Government agencies: NHTSA, DOI, IIHS, FBI, Census Bureau
- Academic peer-reviewed studies
- Carrier annual reports (10-K filings)
- Court records and official filings

**Tier 2 (Use with Caution - Verify):**
- Industry publications: Insurance Journal, PropertyCasualty360
- Trade associations: III (Insurance Information Institute), NAIC
- Reputable news outlets: WSJ, NYT, Bloomberg (for business news)

**Tier 3 (Prohibited):**
- Social media posts
- Unverified blogs or forums
- Anonymous sources
- Competitor marketing materials
- Self-published studies without peer review

**Data Presentation Rules:**
- Always cite the source with publication date
- Link to original data source
- Explain methodology if complex
- Disclose any limitations or caveats
- Use "approximately" or "estimated" when appropriate

**Example - GOOD:**
> "According to the IIHS 2025 Highway Safety Report, rear-end collisions account for approximately 29% of all crashes. [Link to IIHS report]. The study analyzed 2.3 million crashes across 15 states from 2020-2024."

**Example - BAD:**
> "Studies show that 90% of crashes are the other driver's fault. This means you should always get maximum coverage."

---

### 5. CARRIER & PRODUCT COVERAGE

**Allowed:**
- ✅ Factual coverage explanations
- ✅ Policy comparison frameworks (generic)
- ✅ Coverage gap education
- ✅ "What to look for" guides

**PROHIBITED:**
- ❌ Carrier-specific recommendations ("Allstate is the best")
- ❌ Disparagement of competitors
- ❌ Implied endorsements without disclosure
- ❌ Rebates or inducements
- ❌ "Our insurance" vs. "their insurance" language

**Carrier Disclosure Requirements:**

If agent has carrier affiliation (e.g., Allstate agent):

**Required Footer on ALL Stories:**
> "[Agent Name] is an independent [Carrier] agent. This content is educational and not specific to [Carrier] products unless explicitly stated. Availability and coverage details vary by carrier."

If discussing specific carrier's product:
> "This coverage is offered by [Carrier]. Other carriers may have different terms. [Agent Name] represents [Carrier] in [State]."

**Example - GOOD:**
> "Umbrella insurance provides liability coverage beyond your auto and home policies. Most carriers offer $1-5 million in coverage for $150-300/year. Consider umbrella insurance if:
> - You have significant assets
> - You host guests at your home
> - You have teen drivers
>
> Compare quotes from multiple carriers to find the best rate."

**Example - BAD:**
> "Allstate has the best umbrella insurance in Georgia. Our rates are unbeatable. Click here to get a quote and save big!"

## Source Attribution Requirements (Non-Negotiable)

**Every published story MUST include:**

1. **Primary Source Link** - Clickable URL to original document/article
2. **Source Date** - When the source was published (not when we found it)
3. **Source Type** - Court filing, news article, government report, etc.
4. **Source Credibility** - If lesser-known source, explain why trustworthy

**Database Enforcement:**
```sql
-- Stories cannot be published without source
ALTER TABLE stories
  ADD CONSTRAINT require_source_for_publish
  CHECK (
    status != 'published' OR
    (source_name IS NOT NULL AND source_url IS NOT NULL)
  );
```

**In-Story Citation Format:**

**For Single Source:**
> According to the [Organization Name] [Publication Title] ([Year]), [fact or statistic]. [Hyperlink: "View source"]

**For Multiple Sources:**
> Multiple studies have found [fact]:
> - [Source 1] ([Year]): [specific finding]
> - [Source 2] ([Year]): [specific finding]
> [Hyperlink: "View all sources"]

**For Breaking News:**
> This story is developing. Last updated: [timestamp]. Sources: [list sources].

## Geographic Scoping (Multi-Tenant Requirement)

**Rule:** Content must be tagged with applicable states to prevent showing irrelevant content to users.

**Database Field:**
```sql
ALTER TABLE stories ADD COLUMN applicable_states TEXT[];
```

**Tagging Guidelines:**

| Content Type | Applicable States | Example |
|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| State-specific law | ['GA'] | "Georgia No-Fault Law Changes" |
| Multi-state law | ['GA', 'FL', 'TN'] | "Southeastern States Enact New Insurance Fraud Penalties" |
| National trend | NULL (shows to all) | "National Auto Insurance Costs Rise 8% in 2025" |
| Local accident | ['GA'] + metro_area | "I-285 Construction Increases Collision Risk in Atlanta" |

**Display Logic:**
- Users in Georgia see: GA stories + national stories
- Users in Florida see: FL stories + national stories
- Multi-state agencies see: [their licensed states] + national stories

**Why This Matters:**
Showing a Georgia law change to a Florida customer is confusing and reduces trust. Geographic scoping ensures relevance.

## Pre-Publish Compliance Checklist

**Required before `status = 'published'`:**

- [ ] **Primary source cited with URL** (non-negotiable)
- [ ] **Source date included** (publication date)
- [ ] **No prohibited keywords detected** (see auto-block list)
- [ ] **Geographic scope tagged** (applicable_states field)
- [ ] **Fact vs. opinion clearly separated** (use "In my experience..." for opinion)
- [ ] **Legal disclaimer included** (if legal/claims topic)
- [ ] **Carrier disclosure added** (if applicable)
- [ ] **Spelling/grammar reviewed** (Grammarly or similar)
- [ ] **Compliance reviewer assigned** (for Tier 1 risk content)
- [ ] **SEO metadata complete** (meta_title, meta_description)

**Automated Validation (Future Implementation):**
```javascript
// lib/complianceValidator.js
export function validateStoryCompliance(story) {
  const errors = [];

  // Check 1: Source required
  if (!story.source_name || !story.source_url) {
    errors.push("BLOCKING: Missing source attribution");
  }

  // Check 2: Prohibited keywords
  const prohibited = [
    "you should sue",
    "deny the offer",
    "low-ball",
    "commit fraud",
    "inflate your claim"
  ];
  const bodyLower = story.body.toLowerCase();
  prohibited.forEach(keyword => {
    if (bodyLower.includes(keyword)) {
      errors.push(`BLOCKING: Prohibited phrase detected: "${keyword}"`);
    }
  });

  // Check 3: State scoping for legal content
  if (story.category === 'law' && !story.applicable_states) {
    errors.push("WARNING: Legal content should specify applicable states");
  }

  // Check 4: Carrier disclosure
  if (bodyLower.includes('allstate') && !bodyLower.includes('disclosure')) {
    errors.push("WARNING: Carrier mention requires disclosure");
  }

  return errors;
}
```

## Content Lifecycle & Workflow

```
Draft → Self-Edit → Review → Compliance Check → Published
                                    ↓
                                Flagged → Re-Edit → Review
```

**Roles & Permissions:**

| Role | Draft | Edit Own | Edit All | Publish | Compliance Review |
|------|-------|----------|----------|---------|-------------------|
| Viewer | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Editor | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Compliance | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ |
| Admin | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

**Service Level Agreements (SLAs):**
- Draft → Review: 24 hours
- Review → Compliance: 48 hours (for flagged content)
- Compliance → Published/Flagged: 72 hours
- Total cycle time: 3-5 days from draft to publish

**Expedited Publishing (Breaking News):**
- Admin can fast-track high-priority stories
- Still requires compliance checklist completion
- Post-publish review within 24 hours

## Ongoing Monitoring & Audits

### Post-Publish Quality Assurance

**Random Sampling:**
- Audit 10% of published stories quarterly
- Select randomly across all categories
- Review against checklist
- Document findings in `compliance_audits` table

**User-Reported Flags:**
- Add "Report Inaccuracy" button on story pages
- Triage within 24 hours
- Investigate within 48 hours
- Correct or respond within 72 hours

**Analytics-Triggered Reviews:**
Stories flagged if:
- High bounce rate (>80% within 10 seconds)
- Low engagement (<30 seconds avg time on page)
- Negative comments/feedback
- External citation in legal context (Google Alerts)

### Annual External Legal Review

**Scope:**
- Review sample of 50 stories (10 from each category)
- Assess regulatory compliance
- Check for E&O insurance exposure
- Update editorial standards based on findings

**Deliverable:**
Signed legal opinion letter for PE diligence

## Enforcement & Violations

### Violation Severity Tiers:

**Tier 1 - MINOR (Warning):**
- Missing source date (but source URL present)
- Typos or grammatical errors
- SEO metadata incomplete
- Late submissions (past SLA)

**Consequence:** Verbal warning, re-training on specific issue

---

**Tier 2 - MODERATE (Story Unpublished):**
- Unverified statistic
- Unclear opinion vs. fact separation
- Missing geographic scope
- Prohibited keyword in draft (caught in review)
- Source present but low quality (Tier 3)

**Consequence:**
- Story unpublished immediately
- Mandatory re-training
- Review all recent stories by editor
- Probation (next violation escalates)

---

**Tier 3 - SEVERE (Editor Suspended):**
- Legal advice provided
- Prohibited keyword in published story
- Intentional misrepresentation
- Plagiarism
- Failure to correct after user flag

**Consequence:**
- Editor suspended pending investigation
- Legal review of all their content
- Potential termination
- E&O insurance notification

---

**Platform-Wide (Multi-Tenant):**
- Agencies with 3+ Tier 2 violations in 6 months → Newsroom access paused
- Agencies with 1 Tier 3 violation → Immediate suspension, legal review
- Repeat violations → Subscription termination

## Case Studies & Examples

### Case Study 1: Litigation Story Done Right

**Topic:** Georgia Supreme Court rules on diminished value claims

**Published Story Excerpt:**
> "The Georgia Supreme Court ruled unanimously in *Smith v. ABC Insurance Co.* (2024-SC-001234) that insurers must pay diminished value claims within 30 days of receiving a third-party appraisal. [Link to court opinion].
>
> **What This Means for Georgia Drivers:**
> If your car is repaired after an accident but worth less than before (diminished value), you can file a claim with the at-fault driver's insurer. The court's ruling clarifies the timeline for payment.
>
> **Important Notes:**
> - This applies to third-party (at-fault driver's insurer) claims, not your own carrier
> - You must provide a professional appraisal
> - The insurer can dispute the appraisal amount (which may lead to negotiation or mediation)
>
> **What This Doesn't Mean:**
> This ruling doesn't guarantee payment or specific dollar amounts. Each claim is evaluated individually.
>
> For questions about your specific situation, consult an attorney experienced in insurance claims.
>
> Sources:
> - Georgia Supreme Court Opinion 2024-SC-001234 [Link]
> - Georgia DOI Consumer Guide to Diminished Value [Link]"

**Why This Passed Review:**
- ✅ Clear source citation with link
- ✅ Explains ruling without giving legal advice
- ✅ Sets proper expectations
- ✅ Includes attorney referral
- ✅ Multiple credible sources

---

### Case Study 2: Accident Story That Failed Review

**Topic:** Major I-85 pileup, multiple injuries

**Draft Submitted (REJECTED):**
> "A massive 15-car pileup on I-85 yesterday was clearly caused by the truck driver who was speeding. Witnesses say he was going at least 90 mph. The families should definitely sue for millions. If you were involved, your claim is probably worth $50,000-100,000 based on similar cases."

**Why This Failed:**
- ❌ Fault attribution without official determination
- ❌ Unverified witness statements
- ❌ Legal advice ("should sue")
- ❌ Claim valuation
- ❌ No official sources

**Revised Story (APPROVED):**
> "A 15-vehicle collision on I-85 near Exit 42 resulted in 8 injuries yesterday afternoon, according to Georgia State Patrol. [Link to GSP press release].
>
> The crash occurred around 3:45 PM in the northbound lanes. All injured parties were transported to [Hospital] with non-life-threatening injuries.
>
> GSP is investigating the cause. The official accident report is expected within 10 business days.
>
> **If You Were Involved:**
> Georgia law requires you to:
> - Exchange information with other drivers
> - Report the accident to your insurer within a reasonable time (typically 24-72 hours)
> - Obtain a copy of the police report (available at [link])
>
> Your insurer will guide you through the claims process. For questions about fault determination or liability, consult an attorney.
>
> Traffic on I-85 North was diverted for approximately 4 hours. Normal flow resumed by 8 PM.
>
> Sources:
> - Georgia State Patrol Press Release [Link]
> - GDOT Traffic Updates [Link]"

**Why This Passed:**
- ✅ Fact-based reporting from official sources
- ✅ No fault attribution
- ✅ Educational information only
- ✅ Attorney referral for legal questions
- ✅ Clear sourcing
